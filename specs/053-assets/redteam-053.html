Red-team · concern 053

Does concern 053 hold up — and does it help 049 and 050?

Two independent adversarial passes over 053-concern-app-model-and-verbs.md: does resolving it close anything on the specs it claims to help, and does the concern itself survive contact with the specs it cites.

Two answers, both negative, both actionable.

1. Resolving 053 does not de-risk 049/050. Of the 15 red-team findings on those specs, 0 are dissolved, 2 are answered, 12 are untouched, and 1 is made worse. None of the four blockers is helped; the symlink-vs-resolved-path blocker gains two new consumers while staying undecided. 053's real value is narrower than claimed: it forces the rebase-or-accept-rework question to be answered deliberately instead of discovered mid-build.

2. The concern rests on two false premises. Both are load-bearing, both were re-verified by hand against the cited specs, and both are authoring errors in 053 rather than disagreements of judgement: the claim that spec-026's app-name param never reaches argv (it does — systemctl restart {app-name}, with sudo -n), and the claim that portless workloads are structurally invisible so declared apps are the only route to seeing them (two non-port-driven discoverers already exist).

Everything else holds. The 036 hashing citations, the 040 quotes, 049's Known Gaps, 050's verb-by-name inference and 015's CUSTOM-keyed pinning are all verbatim-accurate, and axes B/C/D/F are genuinely distinct. The skeleton is sound; two of its load-bearing bones are not.

Blockers in 053

blocker

Option A1's whole advantage rests on a property the precedent doesn't have

053 · false cross-spec claim

053 describes 026's reserved app-name param as pure correlation that never reaches the command line, and sells option A1 on exactly that: “gate-safe by construction — never reaches argv, so S4 is untouched.” But 026 binds the param straight into argv in three of its own actions, one of them under sudo -n. The sentence was lifted from spec-050, where it is true of 050's lifecycle actions and the 026(d) redeploy shape, and generalised to the whole precedent.

026's real gate-safety argument is different and weaker for 053's purposes: every bound value is validated against ParamBinder.APP_NAME_PATTERN, plus ALLOWED_SET membership. If declarations become reserved params, nothing in the precedent stops a template referencing them — which is precisely what axis B (hash or not) turns on.

Question: if declarations are reserved params, is “never referenced by an argv token” a new invariant to enforce — and where? And what does it mean for 026's existing actions, whose app-name is an argv token?
Re-verified by hand: specs/026-done-app-ops-recipes.md lines 88–95 and 43/61–63.

blocker

“Portless is invisible, so declared apps are the only route” — two discoverers say otherwise

053 · framing error

053's second consequence, and the justification for Decision 4, is that nothing detects a workload that doesn't listen on a port. That describes the monitor chain (025 → 049 → 050) only. 026's SystemdDiscoverer enumerates running units with no port involvement whatsoever and gives them lifecycle ops today; 033 makes the same point for containers explicitly. A systemd-managed queue worker is visible right now.

The decision itself survives — the gap is real — but it is far narrower than stated: hand-launched and cron-scheduled native, non-systemd work. And narrowing it exposes a question 053 never asks: how does a declared app reconcile with the same workload already discovered by systemd or a compose label?

Question: what is the population no existing discoverer reaches — and what happens when a declared app and a discovered app describe the same workload?
Re-verified by hand: 026 line 89 (systemctl list-units --type=service --state=running) and 033 line 31.
Evidence — the claim and the two counter-examples
“Portless native workloads are structurally invisible. A cron script, a queue worker, a batch job, a bash script per se — nothing listens, so nothing detects it.” … “This is the only route by which portless workloads ever become visible.” 053 · Problem consequence 2, and Decision 4

Risks in 053

risk

The biggest question the spec must answer is never asked

053 · open-question gap

Decision 4 introduces an app that exists with no process, and (per Open Question 8) possibly no machine. Nothing in the document asks where such an app persists, what its identity key is, or what the monitor reads to render “declared — no process seen”. Meanwhile the model section forecloses the obvious answer — “never a table” — in the same breath, and 022 explicitly kept that door open as “later work”. Under “never a table”, a declared app with no commands has nothing to be a row of.

Question: what is the persistence substrate of a declared app — and if the answer is any durable record keyed by app name, in what sense is that not the App entity 022 deferred?
Evidence — the foreclosure and what 022 actually said
“app — a name. A namespace, not an entity — spec-022's declined App entity stays declined.” … “an app stays a name plus declarations, never a table.” 053 · model block and Related
“deliberately not a first-class App JPA entity … promoting to an entity is explicitly later work if loose correlation proves insufficient” specs/022-done-monitoring-foundations.md
risk

Is declaring an app a gated act? 052 says it would have to be

053 · unsupported premise

Decision 4 lets “a human or an agent” declare an app, and no part of the document says whether that declaration passes the approval gate. That collides with the invariant 053's own sibling concern states as non-negotiable. Separately, the claim that an agent “cannot declare an app … currently impossible” overstates: 008's AddRecipeTool/AddActionTool plus 026's declarable app-name already form an embryonic route — and 050 records it being used.

Question: is declaring an app proposed-then-approved like every other agent-caused effect, or gate-free metadata — and why is that not an open question?
Evidence
“An app may exist because a human or an agent declared it, with no detected process behind it” 053 · Decision 4
“MCP gains no approve tool. The agent proposes; only the UI decides. Any option that lets an agent both request and release a transfer is out.” specs/052-concern-mcp-file-transfer-gate.md · Notes
“exactly what the demo harness did via MCP — registering run.sh/kill.sh as custom actions and approving them in the UI” 050 · Context — the route 053 calls impossible
risk

Decision 3 settles an option the document elsewhere keeps open

053 · decision masquerading

Decision 3 states the closed verb vocabulary “and are what MCP speaks” — that is option F1, asserted as decided. Option F and Open Question 6 still present F1 versus F2 as a live choice. The eventual spec author cannot tell whether verb-level MCP tools are mandated. Decision 3's own rationale (“keeps ‘what can I do with this app’ a closed question a model can rely on”) only pays off under F1, which deepens the ambiguity rather than resolving it.

Question: was F decided? If Decision 3 fixes only the vocabulary and not the tool shape, say so; if it fixes both, retire F1/F2.

risk

“Violated by construction” turns a design question into a foregone conclusion

053 · unsupported premise

049's gap bans the paths. A name derived from a path is a different object, and 028 — the S9 decision of record — already tolerated exactly that shape for machines: the migration seeds name = host, on the reasoning that a name the owner accepted is not raw infra echoed as coordinates. 053 asserts the collision as settled and pushes toward “naming becomes a human/agent act at approval time” as if forced.

Question: does the S9 line ban path-derived names, or only paths — and does 028's host-seeded machine-name precedent settle it?

The absorption of 040 is only half done — its BFF question is orphaned

053 claims to restate 040's Open Question 1. B3 splits four declarations and is silent on the fifth — the one that carries the argument

053 · option-space gap

The command model declares five things: app-name, part, verb, role, dedication. Option B3 assigns identity (app-name, part) to the hash and presentation (role, dedication) out of it — and never places verb, which is exactly the one B2's own objection singles out (“a verb is now a thing that changes what a button does without passing the gate”).

risk

“Three partial answers that never meet” — they do meet, by design

053 · framing error

033 makes the project name be the appName and keys native-vs-docker dedup on it; 022 deliberately unifies double-detected apps “under one app card by appName string match”, with normalisation rules and a planned alias map. That is an answer to what an app is — a thin one. The real gaps 053 argues (verbs, probe selection, MCP, the narrow portless residue) do not need the stronger claim, and the stronger claim invites a spec that rebuilds a join which already exists.

Question: is anything lost by restating this as “the answers meet only on a loose string, with no verb, probe or MCP semantics”?

Four new conflicts 053 introduces

new conflict

Declared apps break the pipeline they are meant to extend

049's probe fans out over APP_PORT_LIST with the validated port as its sole param; 050's scan takes PID and appRoot as its only arguments and derives managedBy from the cgroup. A declared app has no port, no PID, no cgroup. And 050's control semantics — “Start runs inline and the card flips UP” — assume port-driven liveness, so a portless worker (the very workload Decision 4 exists for) can never flip UP. “Until one of its commands proves otherwise” names a liveness mechanism no spec defines. Decision 4 creates a class of app the 049/050 pipeline structurally cannot serve — new work, not removed work.

new conflict

“Output contract changes” contradicts a decision 049 already made

049 states: “No backend model change. No Flyway migration, no new DTO fields, monitor/ untouched”, and its NDJSON schema carries no app-name/part/role. 053's Decision 2 stamps declarations onto the proposed action, which must persist somewhere. Under option A2 the contradiction is direct; under A1 the NDJSON schema both specs treat as settled still gets rewritten.

new conflict

The undecided path token gains two more masters

Before 053, the symlink-vs-resolved choice had one consumer: 015's hash. Under 053 it has three — the pinned argv, path-derived app-name stability (a releases/123 path changes every deploy; a symlink doesn't), and S9 sanitisation. Under option B1 a renamed app requires re-approval, compounding the redeploy churn the original blocker identified.

new conflict

The closed vocabulary is wider than 050's mapping and misaligned with 026's names

050 renders controls for start/stop/restart/deploy only — status, logs and metrics have no rendering rule. And 050's systemd dedup-by-verb must match 026's existing actions, which are named status/restart/tail-logs, against a vocabulary that says logs. That is rework to 026 that 053's Impact section never mentions.

Method & provenance

Two independent adversarial passes, both document-analysis only — no source, tests or config were read, so this judges the documents as written. Both ran on Fable 5 as separate reviewers with no shared context, one asked to classify the earlier findings against 053, the other to attack 053 with concern-appropriate lenses and to verify seven named claims one by one.

The two blockers and three of the supporting facts were then re-verified by hand against specs/026, specs/033, specs/022 and a repo-wide search for the mis-cited string, because they contradict claims made confidently earlier in the drafting of 053. Both survived that check.

Standing caveat on the review type

The redteam command's own rules say to skip concerns, since “undecided-as-decided” and “buildability gap” do not apply to an exploratory document. That rule was deliberately overridden here at the author's request, with the lenses swapped for ones a concern can actually fail: framing errors, false cross-spec claims, option-space gaps, decisions masquerading as settled, unsupported premises, absorption errors, and open-question coverage.

Read-only. Nothing was written or changed by either pass; every finding names a question rather than a fix.
